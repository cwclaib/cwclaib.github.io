Old MacDonald

Despite doing my own research, I've yet to find a better origin story for NC State's band playing "Old MacDonald" after wins than the following post, written in 2013 by an unnamed band alumnus from the 1970s and posted to PackPower in 2017 by moderator Wolfer79:

Sent: 1/12/2013

For many years I've read various NCSU forums but rarely post. Since you are a moderator and since some people evidently want to know about the band playing "OldMacDonald", I will tell you the story as I remember it. If you decide to do so, feel free to post my message.

As a member of the stage band from 72-76, I was there when we started performing "Old MacDonald" at the end of basketball games. Don Adcock was the band director. Sadly, he passed away last year or he could have perhaps added to this story. We were a real stage band---not the pep band that exits today. Trumpets, trombones, saxes, a bass and a drummer. Think Stan Kenton and Glenn Miller. In addition to popular tunes of the day, the Red & White, and the fight song, we played lots of big band music at basketball games. There were about 18 of us, all guys, though there were girls at State in various musical organizations at the time. The reason there were no girls in the stage band was simply that none of the very best players at NCSU then were female.

"Old MacDonald" was started as a response to the Maryland band. It had nothing to do with Carolina as one poster stated. As some may recall, Maryland was our #1 rivalry in basketball in the early 70s. Of course we caught crap from Duke and UNC fans directing toward us the usual agriculture and idiot jokes but Maryland was the arrogant, hated school. Coach Lefty Drisell referred to Maryland to as the "UCLA of the east". After each Maryland victory, their band would play "Amen", a tune they still play.

In order to rub it in to Maryland and all the teams who ridiculed NCSU students and student athletes, several people inthe band had the idea to ad lib "Old MacDonald". It was our way of saying that your team just lost to a bunch of poor, red-neck farmers. It was basically a response to the sterotype where other schools thought their students and universities were better. There was no sheet music---we just started playing the tune after victories. I do not remember whose idea it was to do this. It may have been Mr. Adcock's idea but it seems to me it was just a spontaneous act at the end of a ball game---probably thehome game against Maryland on January 31, 1973. I can't be sure of the exact date or circumstances but I was a proud member of the stage band that began the tradition 40 years ago.

As for UNC, one of Mr. Adcock's fondest memories was of Dean Smith complimenting our stage band in the early 70s. Coach Smith loved big band music and really liked our sound. I have no recollection of the UNC band making fun of us at anytime.

This recounting largely aligns with what I've found. One minor addition: it may have also evolved from opposing band's playing the song mockingly. Articles from 1973 and 1975 note opposing teams' bands' playing Old MacDonald -- the latter specifically during the NC State-UNC Women's Basketball game in Chapel Hill. By January 1977, the tune was a "favorite" of crowds at basketball games, and was further attested in December 1977. It had migrated to football by November 1978, and by March 1988 it was an oft-requested staple amongst the band's repertoire. Perhaps giving creedence to the UNC origin of the story was a 1987 article by Bruce Phillips, who wrote that "Anytime the teams play in Kenan Stadium, the Carolina band strikes up 'Old MacDonald Had a Farm'" before noting that "Once, the Carolina cheerleaders dressed like farmers and led a pig instead of a ram onto the field." Paying homage to the ad hoc origins of the song, the band still did not formally practice the song (at least, when I was in band from 2017-2021).
